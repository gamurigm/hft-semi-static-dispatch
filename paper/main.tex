\IEEEtitleabstractindextext{%
\begin{abstract}
In High-Frequency Trading (HFT) systems, branch misprediction penalties represent a critical source of non-deterministic latency in the execution critical path. This paper implements and experimentally validates the semi-static conditions technique proposed by Bilokon et al. (2025) through \texttt{FastBranch}, a C++17 header-only abstraction that decouples condition evaluation from the critical flow. For multi-threaded concurrent environments, a lock-free and cache-aware scheme is designed, applying cache-line isolation (\texttt{alignas(64)}) and synchronization based on \textit{release/acquire} semantics to mitigate false sharing. Empirical validation on x86-64 hardware with $5\times10^8$ operations using bootstrap resampling statistical analysis ($B=10\,000$) demonstrated a $1.59\times$ speedup (empirical 95\% CI: $[1.46\times, 1.72\times]$, Cohen's $d=1.07$, $p_{\text{boot}} < 0.0001$), reducing latency from 15.93 ns/op to 10.02 ns/op, consistently converging to the branchless theoretical limit (9.50 ns/op). Negative findings are also documented, including performance degradation due to MESI contention in concurrent writer threads.
\end{abstract}
