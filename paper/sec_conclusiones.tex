\section{Conclusions}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \texttt{FastBranch} achieves a mean latency of 4.57 ns/op, successfully converging to the theoretical physical limit (3.90 ns/op) with an empirical mean speedup of $3.02\times$ (95\% CI: $[2.90\times, 3.14\times]$, Cohen's $d = 1.82$, $p_{\text{boot}} < 0.0001$).
    \item Temporal dispersion and jitter are significantly reduced ($\sigma \approx 0.87$ ns compared to 1.88 ns for the \texttt{switch}), equipping the branching engine with a level of determinism that is critical for market microstructure trading.
    \item The use of raw function pointers coupled with physical cache-line isolation (\texttt{alignas(64)}) and lock-free semantics in C++17 eliminates the penalty of inter-thread false sharing, enabling multi-core scalability without hot-path contention.
    \item The semi-static technique is validated as counterproductive compared to the hardware BPU only when the condition stability exceeds 99.9\% ($f_{\text{set}} \le 1/10^4$), confirming its superiority in highly volatile and unpredictable market scenarios.
\end{enumerate}
